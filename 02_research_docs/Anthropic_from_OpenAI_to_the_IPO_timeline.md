Anthropic, from OpenAI to the IPO

Every headline event that explains where Anthropic came from, what it decided along the way, and where it stands on September 30, 2026. It starts when the founders were still at OpenAI and runs through the week of the leaked prospectus and the White House accord, alongside the moves by OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta, xAI and Chinese labs, the open-versus-closed fight, and the regulation and export controls that shaped the field.

Each entry gives the date, what happened, what it meant for Anthropic, and one link you can open to check it. Use the "Follow" menu to trace one rival or theme through the whole story. Rings around a dot mark the steps toward recursive self-improvement, AI building the next AI.

Has Anthropic reached recursive self-improvement?

Not fully, by its own account, as of September 30, 2026. Full self-improvement would mean Claude choosing and running the research that produces the next Claude with no human in charge of direction. Anthropic says humans still set the goals. But the signals have stacked up in six steps, and its own outside evaluator puts meaningful odds on the first formal trigger already being close.

2015
The founders at OpenAI

Before Anthropic existed, its founders spent four years at OpenAI building the models that proved scaling works, while Google's DeepMind set the pace in research. The disagreements that split them off started here.

Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Ilya Sutskever, Greg Brockman and others launch OpenAI as a research nonprofit, partly as a counterweight to Google's lead in AI.

What it meant for Anthropic
This is the lab most of Anthropic's founders would join. Its later turn toward commerce is the story they left over.

The moment governments and investors noticed. DeepMind, bought by Google in 2014, was the lab to beat, and OpenAI had been founded months earlier partly as its counterweight.

The paper "Attention Is All You Need" introduces the architecture that every frontier language model, Claude included, still uses.

What it meant for Anthropic
Anthropic's own 2026 account of self-improvement names the Transformer as one of the few true breakthroughs in AI; most progress since has been scaling it up.

Source: arXiv

Jan 23, 2020

Research breakthrough

Scaling laws show bigger models reliably get better

Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish, Tom Brown, Dario Amodei and colleagues show that model performance improves smoothly and predictably with more compute, data and size.
