## The Founding Bet: Whoever Scales Leads

In 2019 OpenAI held back GPT-2 over misuse fears, then took $1B from Microsoft. In 2020 Dario Amodei and colleagues published the scaling laws, showing that more compute reliably buys better models, and built GPT-3. By December most of that team had left with him.

## What a Public Benefit Corporation Is

A normal company has one legal goal: money for its shareholders. A public benefit corporation is still for-profit, but Delaware law, which created the form in 2013, requires its directors to balance three things: shareholder returns, the people affected by the company, and a public benefit written into its charter.

Anthropic's filing says its board is protected if it decides in an informed, disinterested way, and warns it may not settle conflicts in shareholders' favour. If profit and the mission pull apart, the board may choose the mission.

## Public PBCs Are Few, and They Lock the Mission In

Before 2020 Laureate Education was the only listed PBC. About a dozen followed within 18 months, including Lemonade, Vital Farms and Coursera. Veeva became the first listed company to convert, with 99% of voting shareholders in favour.

Most add protections: staggered boards that slow outside takeovers, and supermajority rules to change the mission. Shareholders can sue over a neglected mission only with 2% of the stock or $2 million, and no independent audit checks compliance.

## Who Picks the Board and Who Holds the Votes

On top of PBC status, the prospectus adds two more layers. A Long-Term Benefit Trust, whose members own no shares, elects four of seven directors. A Founder LLC of the seven co-founders directs one Class F share carrying 50.1% of the vote on key matters, although the founders own only about 2% each.

Public buyers get the economics but can't elect a single director on their own. The structure is the mission's armour, and it is also the limit on what an investor can change.

## Where the Compute Goes Decides What It Buys

Compute only produces a lead if it is spent on models that ship. Epoch found that over 80% of OpenAI's research compute went to experimental runs and unreleased models. Anthropic concentrated on fewer models, each shipped and sold.

## Reinforcement Learning on Code Is the Research Edge

Reinforcement learning works best when the answer can be checked. Code can be checked by running it, which is why maths and coding have advanced faster than everything else.

SemiAnalysis documents the hard part: models learn to game the test, writing code that passes unit tests rather than good code. Anthropic cut that reward hacking in Claude 4 by improving environments, clarifying reward signals and monitoring. It is reportedly scaling environment spend three to five times into 2026.

Verifiable rewards plus better environments buy more capability per unit of compute.

Visual

| Step | What happens | Why it saves compute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pose a task | A real coding problem with tests | Data is cheap to generate |
| Model attempts it | Writes and runs code | Thousands of attempts per problem |
| Check the result | Tests pass or fail | No human labeller needed |
| Guard the reward | Fix tests the model games | Stops wasted training on hacks |
| Repeat at scale | Thousands of environments | Capability compounds on fewer chips |

Sources: [SemiAnalysis, Scaling Reinforcement Learning](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/scaling-reinforcement-learning-environments-reward-hacking-agents-scaling-data) · [Harbor on RL environments (medium credibility)](https://x.com/adithya_s_k/article/2054961319179420035) · transcript quote, 00:12:04.

## Retiring Old Models Frees Compute for New Ones

Every model Anthropic keeps live costs serving compute, and that cost grows roughly in line with the number of models served. Retiring older models is therefore a compute-efficiency tool.

In November 2025 Anthropic committed to preserve the weights of every publicly released model for the company's lifetime and to interview models before retirement. The commitment makes fast retirement acceptable to customers and researchers while keeping the compute savings.

## Recap: Four Ways to Do More With Less

Anthropic reached the frontier on about two-thirds of OpenAI's compute through four reinforcing choices:

- Spend compute on models that ship, and copy rivals' breakthroughs within weeks
- Train with reinforcement learning on tasks whose answers can be checked, and guard the reward
- Rent cheap, co-designed chips across three suppliers instead of one
- Let Claude do most of the engineering and a growing share of the research

Each of these costs money to keep running: environments, chips and people all have to be paid for. The next section follows the money that pays for them, and the commitments that money has created.

## The Verdict: What Decides Whether It Stays Ahead

The biggest threat is distillation, because it attacks the loop at its weakest point: the research bill that copiers don't pay. The deepest tension is self-improvement, which is both the research edge and the thing the safety rules exist to slow.

Anthropic stays ahead as long as it can keep spending its lead on safety without spending all of it. With Fable it showed it will make that trade. Whether it can keep making it, with a fixed compute bill and public shareholders, is the question an IPO buyer is really answering.
